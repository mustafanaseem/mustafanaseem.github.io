<p class="about-intro">I'm Mustafa Naseem, a Clinical Associate Professor at the University of Michigan School of Information. I study how technology can expand access to health information and support, and how gender norms and institutional practices shape its use. My work combines field research, system design, and experiments, primarily in Pakistan and with refugee communities in the United States.</p>

<h2 class="about-h2">Research and Impact</h2>

<p>I have spent much of my career working with public institutions to develop services they can sustain and expand. The Punjab government adopted a modified version of our team's immunization card design for use across the province. At Michigan, I was the initial UX research and design lead for the <a href="https://www.mistartmap.info/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">MI Safe Start Map</a>, a dashboard developed to inform the state government's COVID-19 reopening decisions.</p>

<p>These collaborations have also supported research on how public services operate. Our fieldwork documented the <a href="https://mustafanaseem.com/files/chi2021_intentioanldatafalsification-2.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">methods vaccination supervisors developed to detect falsified records</a>. With Super Abbu, we ran experiments to establish <a href="https://mustafanaseem.com/files/chi2020_superabbuads-7.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">which recruitment approaches brought people to a voice-based health service and kept them engaged</a>. In our <a href="https://mustafanaseem.com/files/2024_NCIDA%20Image%20Abuse.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">research on image-based abuse</a>, we showed how threats involving non-sexual photographs could expose women to harm from family members, broadening what existing definitions of abuse accounted for.</p>

<p>I want the systems we build to be useful, and I want the research to explain why they work, where they fail, and what our field needs to reconsider. My current projects extend this work to <a href="https://globalhealthequity.umich.edu/research/supported-projects/addressing-technology-facilitated-GBV" target="_blank" rel="noopener">technology-facilitated gender-based violence</a>, <a href="https://www.si.umich.edu/about-umsi/news/three-umsi-researchers-earn-google-academic-research-awards-combat-ai-scams" target="_blank" rel="noopener">AI-enabled scams</a>, and <a href="https://umctw.wordpress.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">community digital support</a>. The <a href="{{ base_path }}/impact/">Impact page</a> describes the services and interventions that have developed from this research.</p>

<h2 class="about-h2">Teaching and mentorship</h2>

<p>I want students to develop confidence in their own judgment and feel comfortable coming to me when they struggle. My parents expected a lot of me and made sure I had support along the way. I bring that approach to teaching through detailed feedback and opportunities to revise work.</p>

<blockquote class="about-quote">
<p>"Though the material in this course was sometimes fairly challenging, I never felt lost or helpless."</p>
<cite>&mdash; Student course evaluation, IT &amp; Global Society, Fall 2019</cite>
</blockquote>

<p>I created Humanitarian Innovation: Co-Design for Social Impact to give students sustained experience collaborating with Afghan community members. Community consultants guide each team's work, while shared meals and exercises on power and bias help students examine their assumptions.</p>

<p>One-on-one mentorship is among the most rewarding parts of my work. Some students I have mentored now hold tenure-track faculty positions, and I have had the privilege of learning from them throughout our collaborations.</p>

<p><a href="https://www.michigandaily.com/michigan-in-color/how-professor-mustafa-naseem-is-changing-the-world-one-app-at-a-time/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Read Eliya Emtiaz's profile of my teaching and research in The Michigan Daily.</a></p>

<h2 class="about-h2">Learning through co-design</h2>

<p>From 2009 to 2018, I took part in the International Development Design Summit (IDDS), progressing from participant to organizer and instructor. I co-led three summits in Botswana (2015, 2016, and 2018) and was lead organizer of the 2016 Pakistan summit, where Super Abbu began.</p>

<p>The summits I helped organize brought together people from more than 20 countries, aged 17 to 82, with education ranging from unfinished secondary school to postdoctoral research. Facilitating these collaborations shaped my approach to co-design: adapting activities to different languages and literacy levels, addressing how gender and status affect whose ideas are heard, and sharing design decisions while respecting individual dignity and cultural context. These methodological concerns also inform our <a href="https://mustafanaseem.com/files/compass2022_powerdifferentials-4.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">research on power differentials and cultural differences in co-design</a>, which examines both the strategies facilitators use and the limits of what a workshop can change.</p>

<h2 class="about-h2">Institution Building</h2>

<p>As one of the founding faculty at Information Technology University in Lahore, I helped establish the computer science department and develop its academic policies. This included undergraduate admissions, master's thesis assessment, and disciplinary procedures. Establishing these policies was a substantial part of my early faculty responsibilities, alongside teaching and developing a research program.</p>

<p>I co-founded the Innovations for Poverty Alleviation Lab (IPAL) with Samia Razaq, now Samia Ibtesam, and led its work on public services, including immunization. I secured &pound;250,000 as principal investigator, and by the time I left for Colorado, the lab had more than 15 full-time research staff. My <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ns8ObdQ1OPE" target="_blank" rel="noopener">2015 talk at re:publica</a> offers a view of our work in Pakistan at the time.</p>

<p>With Max Kr&uuml;ger and Umar Shehzad, I also co-founded Make-i-stan. We wanted people from engineering, computing, and the arts to have somewhere to try things together. By the time I left, the makerspace was receiving roughly 300&ndash;500 visits a month.</p>

<p>I later returned to Colorado Boulder, where I had completed my master's as a Fulbright Scholar, as ICTD Expert-in-Residence. There, I helped run the master's program through recruitment, advising, and course planning. I joined Michigan in 2018, have served on the dean's advisory committee, and completed my PhD in Design Science in 2025. My <a href="https://mustafanaseem.com/files/Mustafa%20Naseem%20CV_Dec25.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">CV</a> has the fuller record of research, teaching, and service.</p>

<h2 class="about-h2">Bio for press and events</h2>

<p>Mustafa Naseem is a Clinical Associate Professor at the University of Michigan School of Information. His research in human-computer interaction examines how people access information and use technology, and how social relationships and institutions shape those experiences. Combining field research, participatory design, and experiments, he works with communities and public institutions in Pakistan and the United States. His projects span public health, digital safety, and community technology support, with several adopted by government or reaching tens of thousands of people. His teaching emphasizes research skills and collaboration with communities, and he mentors students beginning their research careers. He has helped establish academic programs, research labs, and spaces for collaborative design. His work has received support from NSF, NIH, the Gates Foundation, USAID, UNICEF, and Google. He holds a PhD in Design Science from Michigan and a master's degree from the University of Colorado Boulder, where he was a Fulbright Scholar.</p>
